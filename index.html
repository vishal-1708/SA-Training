AI & Machine Learning

How Large Language Models Are Reshaping IT Job Descriptions in 2025

By Senthil Kumar A.  ·  November 12, 2025  ·  8 min read

In 2022, a job listing for "Python Developer" typically mentioned Django, REST APIs, and Git. By mid-2025, the same role title frequently lists LLM integration, vector databases, and prompt engineering as requirements. The shift has been fast, uneven, and largely unreported in mainstream education circles.

At SA Training, we track job description language across 500+ postings from our hiring partners every quarter. Here's what that data shows — and what it means for anyone planning their IT career in Tamil Nadu or beyond.

The Three Skill Layers Now Expected of Developers

Hiring managers in our network consistently describe a "three-layer" expectation that has emerged since 2023. First, classical software engineering fundamentals — data structures, system design, clean code — remain non-negotiable. Second, cloud-native fluency: knowing how to deploy, monitor, and scale services on AWS or Azure is now a baseline, not a differentiator. Third, and most recently, AI-augmented development: the ability to integrate LLM APIs, build RAG pipelines, or fine-tune models for specific use cases.

What's notable is that the third layer hasn't replaced the first two — it has stacked on top of them. The developers earning the highest offers aren't pure AI specialists; they're engineers who understand classical software design and can reason about where AI tools belong in a system.

What This Means for Your Learning Path

If you're starting from scratch, we still recommend beginning with strong programming foundations — Python, data structures, one web framework. The developers who struggle with AI tooling most aren't those who learned ML late; they're those who tried to shortcut the fundamentals. Build the foundation solid, then layer in cloud and AI knowledge on top of a working mental model of how software systems behave.

What 500 Hiring Managers in Tamil Nadu Actually Look for in Freshers

By Ramya Priya S.  ·  October 28, 2025  ·  6 min read

Last year, SA Training surveyed 500 hiring managers across Tamil Nadu — from Coimbatore's manufacturing sector tech teams to Chennai's startup ecosystem. The results challenged several assumptions we had about what entry-level hiring actually looks for.

The most common complaint from hiring managers wasn't lack of technical knowledge. It was the inability to debug independently, communicate progress during a task, and write readable code that others can maintain. These are metacognitive and professional skills — and most college programmes don't teach them explicitly.

The top five things hiring managers actually tested in fresher interviews, in order: (1) problem decomposition — can you break a vague problem into concrete steps? (2) code readability and naming conventions; (3) Git workflow fluency; (4) the ability to explain a technical decision out loud; (5) basic SQL querying under time pressure.

At SA Training, we've built these explicitly into every course — not as an afterthought, but as the frame through which every technical concept is taught. Our students don't just learn what a function does; they learn to explain why they wrote it the way they did.

Cloud & Infrastructure

AWS vs Azure in 2025: Which Certification Actually Opens More Doors?

By Anjali K.  ·  October 10, 2025  ·  7 min read

The AWS vs Azure debate comes up in every cloud counselling session we run at SA Training. Both platforms are legitimate, both certifications are well-regarded — but the right choice depends on where you want to work, not which platform is globally dominant.

In Tamil Nadu specifically, the landscape looks like this: manufacturing and government-adjacent companies have been heavier Azure adopters, partly due to Microsoft's existing enterprise relationships and Office 365 integrations. Product-focused startups and GCCs (Global Capability Centres) lean AWS heavily. If you're aiming at MNCs with Indian development centres — TCS Digital, Cognizant, Infosys BPM — AWS certification has a slight edge in raw job posting volume.

That said, the most competitive candidates we place aren't single-cloud. The fundamentals — networking, IAM, storage, compute, serverless, containers — transfer between platforms. We recommend starting with AWS for breadth, earning the Solutions Architect Associate certification, and then acquiring an Azure Fundamentals cert as a second signal. The combination costs roughly six months of focused study and dramatically widens the opportunity set.

What we tell every cloud student: the certification is proof you studied. The projects you build during the course are proof you can work. Both matter. Neither alone is enough.

Cybersecurity Careers in India: Why 2025 Is the Right Time to Enter the Field

By Murugan V.  ·  September 22, 2025  ·  5 min read

India reported a 25% year-on-year increase in cybersecurity incidents in 2024. The government's Digital India push, expanding fintech infrastructure, and the rise of cloud-native enterprises have collectively created a talent gap that the country's existing cybersecurity workforce cannot fill.

The numbers are stark. According to NASSCOM, India needs approximately 1 million trained cybersecurity professionals by 2026. Current supply sits well below that. For someone entering the field now, it means the demand side is unusually favourable — entry-level security analyst roles are paying ₹5–8 LPA in metro areas, with certified ethical hackers clearing ₹12 LPA+ at the mid-level.

What makes cybersecurity different from other IT disciplines is that the entry point matters enormously. Many developers pivot to security roles, but those who train specifically for security from the outset — with hands-on penetration testing, familiarity with attack frameworks like MITRE ATT&CK, and exposure to SIEM platforms — tend to advance faster because they think offensively from the start. That's the mindset we build at SA Training from day one of our Ethical Hacking course.

If you're weighing which IT specialisation to pursue, cybersecurity in 2025 combines favourable demand, meaningful work, and genuine career depth. The barrier is real knowledge and practical skill — which is exactly what structured training is for.
